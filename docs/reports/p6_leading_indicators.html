2. Ordinary weeks

The on-time start rate correlates +0.62 with on-time delivery 1 week later, against a 95th percentile of +0.24 on shuffled series and +0.21 on shifted series. No other series exceeds both comparisons with the event weeks excluded.

Figure 1. Correlation of each series with on-time delivery by weeks of lead, event weeks excluded and all weeks; the band is the shuffled 95th percentile.

3. The 2024 year-end build

Jobs waiting at the lasers led on-time delivery by 7 weeks (+0.80): 38 to 144 jobs in the 8 weeks before the decline of December 9, 2024 against 31 to 47 as the quarterly mean before it. The on-time start rate (+0.96 at 1 week), brake backlog at release (+0.92 at lag 0), jobs waiting at the brakes (+0.89 at lag 0) and overtime labor hours at the brakes (+0.82 at lag 0) move with on-time delivery and describe the event under way. Brake standard hours released reaches +0.30 at 12 weeks, the edge of the range tested. In the week of December 22, 2025, jobs waiting at the lasers stood at 134, above the trigger in Table 2.

4. Declines

On-time delivery declined four times in 36 months, in the weeks of August 14, 2023, July 29, 2024, December 9, 2024 and October 13, 2025; thresholds of 3, 4 and 5 points give the same four and 6 points gives three. The on-time start rate moved before all four, by 5, 1, 2 and 7 weeks (median 3.5), against 1.7 expected on shuffled series and 1.7 on shifted (share at or above 0.03 and 0.02). Brake backlog at release, jobs waiting at the lasers and jobs waiting at the brakes each moved before 3 of 4 against 1.6 to 2.3 expected, which is not distinguishable from chance. With four declines the count separates the on-time start rate from the rest and nothing else. The on-time start rate gave 6 signal episodes and 3 were followed by a decline within 8 weeks; brake backlog at release 6 and 3; jobs waiting at the lasers 5 and 3.

Figure 2. On-time delivery with the four declines marked, and the on-time start rate and jobs waiting at the lasers with the weeks each had moved shaded.

5. Brake backlog at release

Jobs released with more than 3 days of brake work waiting are 66.1% of the late jobs released in 2025 against 28.9% of all jobs, and 5.7% against 4.2% in Q2 to Q4. The late rate is 7.4% to 9.5% below 3 days and 14.7%, 36.0%, 35.9% and 53.1% in the bands above. Every job released in the first quarter was above the threshold; in Q2 to Q4 it marks 4.2% of jobs.

Figure 3. Late rate by brake backlog at release, jobs released in 2025 and in Q2 to Q4, with the number of jobs on each bar.

6. Lines promised inside the standard lead time

They are 19.1% of jobs and 57.9% of late jobs released in Q2 to Q4 (late rate 27.3% against 4.7%), and 18.6% and 30.8% for the year. The weekly share is flat at 15% to 21% by quarter and does not lead as a series; the flag identifies the jobs.

7. Kit completeness and material lateness

85.5% of material-caused lost days in 2025 are on jobs with a short kit (95.6% in Q2 to Q4) against a short rate of 31.2% on all kit checks. As a weekly series kit completeness does not lead on-time delivery in ordinary weeks (+0.13).

8. Not leading

Setup efficiency at the brakes (+0.08 in ordinary weeks, +0.02 in all weeks) and outside-processing receipts on time (+0.11, -0.02) do not exceed chance. Schedule adherence has the opposite sign (-0.28 at lag 0 in all weeks): adherence is higher when on-time delivery is lower. Overtime labor hours at the brakes move with on-time delivery (+0.82 at lag 0) and not ahead of it (+0.03 in ordinary weeks).

9. The weekly review set

Two weekly indicators and three job-level flags carry the lead found. One of the two weekly indicators moved 5, 6, 4 and 7 weeks before the four declines, against 2.7 declines expected on shuffled series and 2.8 on shifted (share at or above 0.21 and 0.19); the pair is not distinguishable from chance.

Recommendation

Production control reviews the on-time start rate and jobs waiting at the lasers weekly against the trigger in Table 2, on the dashboard's leading panel. Flag at release every job released with more than 3 days of brake backlog and every line promised inside the standard lead time, and flag a short kit at the kit check. Drop setup efficiency, schedule adherence, outside-processing receipts and overtime from the leading set; they remain measures of their own work centers.

Method and data

Correlations are signed so that positive means the indicator's adverse movement goes with lower on-time delivery; the statistic is the largest correlation at a lead of 1 to 12 weeks. Shuffled, the specified comparison, puts the indicator's weeks in random order; shifted moves the indicator in time by a random 13 weeks or more and keeps each series' week-to-week pattern. Both use 1,000 series.
A decline starts in the first week the on-time delivery of the trailing 4 weeks is more than 5 points below that of the 13 weeks before them, at least 8 weeks after the last. An indicator has moved when its 4-week mean is adverse to the 13 weeks before by more than one standard deviation of those weeks; a decline is preceded when the indicator moved in the 8 weeks before it.
The on-time start rate leaves out the 4,235 of 76,282 in-house operations (5.6%) planned to start before the job's release; they belong to lines promised inside the standard lead time (P7). 1 week shipping fewer than 30 jobs carries no on-time delivery figure. The first four weeks of 2023 are left out of every weekly series: the jobs in process when the records begin have no labor transactions for their earlier operations.
A recorded kit shortage is one trigger of the P2 material rule, so the share of material lost days on short kits is in part the rule's own definition; 7 of 275 jobs with material days have no kit check. Overtime is labor hours on Saturdays and after 22:00 on days with an extended second shift, not the shift counts of P5.
