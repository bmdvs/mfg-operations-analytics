1. Capacity Across the Shop

The shop's floor consists of 25 machines across ten work centers. The press brakes run at the highest utilization of any work center, in every quarter since 2023 but one. The robotic weld cell has been second in every quarter from 2025 Q1, having run at 53% to 79% before then, when the lasers and other work centers ran above it; it passed the brakes once, in 2025 Q3 (92% against 90%).

Utilization by Work Center, 2023 to 2025

In 2025, the press brakes ran at 91% utilization against 97% uptime, and this 6-point gap was the smallest production capacity buffer of any work center. The robotic weld cell ran at 83% utilization against 97% uptime, a 14-point buffer. Uptime was 96% to 98% at every work center; for purposes of this report, uptime treats setup as running time, not downtime. See Appendix Table 1 for additional detail.

Utilization and Machine Uptime by Work Center

Not only do the press brakes and robotic weld cell have the highest average utilization, they also spend more time than any other work station at or near maximum utilization. Shown below is the share of weeks in 2025 that each work station spent at or above 95% average utilization. The press brakes and the robotic weld cell were at this level in 46% and 38% of weeks throughout the year.

Share of weeks at or above 95% utilization, 2025

Work stations experienced an increase in queue time when operating near maximum utilization, compared to weeks at lower utilization. Notably, the press brakes were the exception: their queue time in weeks at or above 95% utilization was lower than in their other weeks (3.06 days against 3.98). This is because the other weeks on this station are still running at 85% to 95% utilization in 19 of 28 cases and carry the backlog of the weeks before them.

Queue Time in Weeks at or Above 95% Utilization and Other Weeks, 2025

Throughout 2025, jobs spent 36,318 days waiting at a work center after their first operation. The press brake station accounted for 56% of those days, which is a function of its consistently high utilization, as well as its volume of operations. There were nearly 5,700 brake operations in 2025. The robotic weld cell, which saw similar queue times to the press brakes (3.64 days per operation against 3.54), accounted for less than 5% of queue days, because it saw only 423 operations in 2025.

2. Load, Queue Time and WIP

Queue time against load

The curve is the queue time expected at each level of utilization for the number of machines at the work center, fitted on rolling 13-week windows from 2023 to 2025; for the brakes the fit explains 45% of the variation in the windowed queue time with a rank correlation of 0.88, while a single week's queue time carries the backlog of the weeks before it. The curve is drawn only over the range of utilization the work center has run at in those windows; it is not extended beyond it. Single weeks at full utilization can show short waits when the backlog behind them is small, which is why the fit is made on windows rather than weeks. Three work centers carry sustained load and follow the curve: the brakes, the robotic weld cell and the lasers. Each is shown below with its weekly record.

Press brake

On the fitted curve the brake queue time is 0.67 days per operation at 75% utilization, 1.51 at 85%, 2.61 at 90% and 3.44 at 92%; the measured queue time is 3.54 at 91% for the year and 1.81 at 91% in Q2-Q4. Week by week, the brake queue time steps from about 0.5 days below 85% utilization to 2.7 above it and does not settle back. The brake queue time's 90th percentile was 17.1 and 9.1 days in Q1 and Q2 2025 and 2.8 and 3.0 in Q3 and Q4; under 5 days in every quarter is the mark the levers in this report are measured against. See Appendix Tables 2 and 3 for additional detail.

Queue Time Against Utilization: Press brake (5 machines), 2023 to 2025

The brakes run at 91% and carry 56% of all queue time (Flow and On-time Delivery). B1 and B2 run at 92.2% and 91.9% for the year and 93.7% and 93.7% in Q2-Q4, with the shortest queue times of the five (mean 2.7 and 2.8 days): precision work goes to them first and the hot list sends expedited work to them. B3 to B5 carry the tail: mean 3.7 to 4.1 days and 90th percentile 11.6 to 12.7. Over all hours worked, B5 carries the highest load of the five for the year; in Q2-Q4 B1 and B2 do. On crewed weekday shifts only, B1 and B2 run at 96.6% and 96.3% for the year. See Appendix Tables 7 and 8 for additional detail.

Halving the spread of brake setup time (each setup time moved halfway to the mean, the curve rescaled for the lower variability of setup and run) releases 0.8 brake hours a week and cuts the queue time on the curve from 3.08 to 2.98 days. The variability at the brakes is in arrivals (daily coefficient of variation 0.50, Mondays 1.77 times the daily mean, the top customer's month-end 1.73 times) and in run time (coefficient of variation 2.14 against 0.89 for setup). This is the spread of setup time, not its mean; the mean is the subject of Section 3. Second-shift absence is 7% against 3% on first shift. See Appendix Tables 9 and 10 for additional detail.

Utilization and Queue Time by Brake, 2025

Robotic weld cell

The robotic weld cell runs at 83% on one shift and has the longest queue time in the shop in Q2-Q4, 3.9 days per operation against 1.8 at the brakes. As a single machine its wait climbs faster with load than the brakes': 5.4 days on the curve at 90% against 2.6 at the five brakes. It runs at or above 95% in 20 weeks of 52 and below 80% in 18, alternating between idle and saturated weeks rather than running steadily hot, and in 2025 it ran above its curve (3.64 days measured against 2.84).

Queue Time Against Utilization: Robotic weld (1 machine), 2023 to 2025

Enclosures are 78% of its hours, weldments 10% and chassis 6%. Its setups run at 1.03 of standard and its run time at 1.06, so the cell's queue is load, not standards. See Appendix Table 11 for additional detail.

A second shift on the robotic weld cell is the open shift on the secondary constraint; the options report tests and prices it.

Lasers

The lasers run at 74% and matter at the year-end peak. Over the six weeks from November 11, 2024 they ran at 84% to 98%, and at 93% to 98% in the first three, with the first-operation queue rising from 0.6 to 4.9 days by mid-December; they reached 95% in 5 of 138 full weeks in three years. The curve fits them over the 52% to 84% range they run at in ordinary quarters (1.31 days measured in 2025 against 1.22 on the curve); above it the record is the build weeks. See Appendix Table 4 for additional detail.

Queue Time Against Utilization: Laser (3 machines), 2023 to 2025

In an ordinary month the first-operation queue time is under a day and a half (0.58 to 1.31 on the monthly mean) and its 90th percentile no more than 4.3 days; January (4.67) and December (2.56) are the two builds. Material wait is steady through the year: 36% of first operations wait for material, 0.7 to 1.1 days on the monthly mean across all jobs and 2.3 to 2.5 days where there is a wait. The work center's 74% hides a split by machine: L1 runs one shift at 91%, L2 and L3 run two shifts at 71% and 72%. L2 and L3 carry 80% of laser standard hours and run them in 0.81 of the standard time, so planned laser hours on the two are overstated by 1,126 for the year (7,472 standard against 6,353 actual across the three machines); Section 4 takes this up. See Appendix Table 13 for additional detail.

Flat, then a step at full load

At four work centers the wait does not rise with load until the machines are full. Assembly, the manual weld bays and the punch sit at 0.20 to 0.84 days per operation below 85% utilization and 2.02 to 3.26 at or above 95%; the lasers in an ordinary quarter show the same floor, with their step the year-end build above. The fitted curve does not describe this shape, which is a flat floor and a step rather than a smooth rise (rank correlation 0.67 to 0.86 on 13-week windows), so the band means are shown instead.

Queue Time by Utilization Band: Assembly, Manual Weld, Punch and Laser, 2023 to 2025

Assembly

Assembly runs at 69% for the year but at 99% in its 11 hot weeks. Its two benches ran at 69% in 2025, up from 52% in 2023 and 60% in 2024, with 11 hot weeks against none in 2023 and 9 in 2024. In the hot weeks utilization is 99% and the queue time 4.35 days per operation against 61% and 1.09 in the other 41; 6 of the 11 are the first-quarter backlog. The hot weeks are assembly's own: arrivals run 14.5 a week against 12.8 and operations are 21% larger (4.37 machine hours against 3.62), while the robotic weld cell's share of arrivals barely moves (19% against 16%) and assembly utilization does not follow the cell's output in the same week or the two before it. Assembly receives 47% of its work from the powder line and 25% from outside processing, the two stages that release work on their own schedules, and the longest assembly queue times are on jobs with no weld operation at all (90th percentile 6.7 days against 3.9 and 5.5). See Appendix Table 12 for additional detail.

Assembly Weekly Utilization and Queue Time

Manual weld

The manual weld bays run at 66% and are flat at 0.74 days per operation below 85% utilization; at or above it they wait 2.10 days, a load the six bays reach in 11 weeks of 52 in 2025. Below that load their queue is the daily list's (next subsection).

Punch

The punch is one machine at 66% running about 16 operations a week, so a few large jobs fill a week; its 10 hot weeks in 2025 raised a short wait from 0.38 to 2.22 days per operation and carried 27% of its hours. Nothing downstream depends on it, and it is left at that.

Set by the daily list

At grind and deburr, inspection and pack and hardware, queue time is flat across the range of utilization the shop has run them at, at 0.65 to 0.88 days per operation for the year whatever the week's load. At these three work centers 52% to 58% of operations start on the working day after they arrive and wait about 0.9 days to do so; 33% to 44% start the same day, within half a day; 4% to 9% wait longer. The split is the same in Q2-Q4 (61% to 67% next day). What decides it is the hour of arrival, not the load: work arriving before 10:00 starts the same day nine times in ten, work arriving between 10:00 and 14:00 (51% of arrivals) starts the same day about three times in ten, and the 24% arriving after the shift ends starts the next morning. The brakes show no such break; 42% to 60% of their arrivals start the same day in every band from 06:00 to 14:00. Across a shipped job, these next-day waits add 1.3 working days of lead time (1.4 in Q2-Q4). See Appendix Table 14 for additional detail.

Queue Time by Utilization Band: Grind and Deburr, Inspection and Pack and Hardware, 2023 to 2025
Same-Day Start by Hour of Arrival, Dispatch-List Work Centers Against the Brakes, 2025

The lever is the list, not capacity. A midday refresh of the day's list at these three work centers lets the arrivals between 10:00 and 12:00, 21% of the total, start the same afternoon; what arrives after the shift ends can only start the next morning.

Set by the color schedule

The powder line's color-day wait is 1.03 days per operation and 32% of operations wait a day or more. Black, white and gray run two days a week and wait 0.66 to 0.88 days; beige, blue and red run once a week, wait 1.8 to 1.9 days, and 58% to 65% of their operations wait a day or more. The three once-a-week colors are 24% of operations and 43% of the wait; black is 26% of the wait on volume alone. A third day for black was tested in the options report and does not move on-time delivery. See Appendix Table 15 for additional detail.

Hot weeks across the floor

A hot week is one in which a work center ran at or above 95% utilization. As seen below, seven work centers recorded at least one hot week in 2025, with the brakes recording the most, followed by the robotic weld cell and assembly. The punch recorded the third lowest average utilization yet 10 hot weeks, because it is a single machine running about 16 operations a week, so a few large jobs fill a week. The lasers recorded the third highest utilization yet only 4 hot weeks, because they pool three machines over about 84 operations a week and go hot only when releases surge, as they did in the six weeks of the Q4 2024 build (Table 4) that formed the backlog detailed in Flow and On-time Delivery. In Q2-Q4 a hot week shows as a longer wait at each of the six that had one: 2.00 days per operation at the brakes against 1.57 in the other weeks, 5.26 against 2.94 at the robotic weld cell, 3.72 against 1.15 at assembly, 1.26 against 0.26 at the punch, 1.99 against 0.83 at the lasers and 2.58 against 0.78 at the manual weld bays. For the full year the brakes read the other way (3.06 against 3.98) because the first-quarter backlog kept the wait long whatever the week's utilization. The count of jobs at the work center rises too, though by less than the wait does: 41 jobs at the brakes against 35 in other weeks and 9.9 against 5.9 at the robotic weld cell. Hot weeks carry 57% of brake hours and 50% of the weld cell's in Q2-Q4, 27% of the punch's and 19% of assembly's. See Appendix Tables 5 and 6 for additional detail.

Queue Time and Jobs at the Work Center in Hot Weeks and Other Weeks, Q2-Q4 2025

The brake queue is set by arrival variability and by load above 85%, so capacity at the constraint is the lever: the setup program of Section 3 and planned Saturday brake shifts for the peak, both tested in Options Tested. Release leveling and dispatch rules do not help this shop, and reducing the spread of setup time is not a lever. At the three list-set work centers the lever is the list; at the powder line it is the color schedule.

3. Setups Against Standard

27,171 setups at all work centers in 2025, 5,354 of them at the brakes. A setup's ratio is its setup machine hours over the setup standard on the job operation; hours over standard is total setup hours over total standard hours. Every work center except the brakes runs its setups at a median of 0.97 to 1.00 of standard and within 44 overrun hours for the year; the brakes ran 6,077 hours against 4,235 standard, 1,842 over, 35 a week, 11% of brake machine time. The median brake setup runs at 1.16 of standard and the 90th percentile at 2.07. Setup ratios do not move with the 2024 year-end build: the median is 1.17 in the first quarter and 1.16 in Q2-Q4. See Appendix Tables 16 and 17 for additional detail.

Where the overrun sits

Setups handed over at a shift boundary (setup transactions by different operators one after the other across the 14:00 or 22:30 shift boundary; two operators at the same time is a two-person setup, not a handover) are 13% of setups and 49% of the overrun hours, median 1.77 against 1.10; most of those hours are long setups that reached the boundary rather than the handover itself (The setup reduction list and the handover). Lots under 25 are 49% of setups and 66% of the overrun, median 1.28 against 1.02 to 1.05 on larger lots: the setup standards under-plan small lots. First setups of a part by the operator run at 1.28 against 1.06. Enclosures and chassis run at 1.33 and 1.29; panels and brackets at 1.06 and 1.07. See Appendix Tables 18, 19, 20 and 21 for additional detail.

Brake Setup Overrun Hours by Condition

The setup standard on lots under 25 pieces should be raised by 24%, so the schedule and the quote stop under-planning them.

Operator tenure and familiarity

Operators with under 12 months run 16% above tenured operators before controls and 5% after familiarity, grouping, machine, lot size, bend count and shift handover. Their 636 setups of 5,354 were made by 4 operators, 400 by one, and 67% were on B4. The tenure effect is mostly what new operators are given, not who they are. See Appendix Table 22 for additional detail.

Tenure Effect on Brake Setup Ratio as Controls Are Added

1,079 first setups of a repeat part were made while another current brake operator had set the part up in the previous 24 months. They ran at 1.56 of standard against 1.28 for setups with prior familiarity in the same lot band and family: 4.4 brake hours a week (3.1 where that operator was on the same shift). This is a matched comparison, not a start-time test, so part of it may be selection. Repeat setups should go to an operator who has set the part up before. See Appendix Table 23 for additional detail.

Grouping

38% of brake setups follow a job on the same tooling set and run at 0.92 of standard against 1.33, saving 5.4 hours a week. B1 and B2 group 26% to 27% of their setups against 42% to 46% on B3 to B5: precision work and the hot list come first there. See Appendix Table 24 for additional detail.

Same-tooling Grouping by Brake

The setup reduction list and the handover

The top 12 part-operations release 7.3 brake hours a week at standard: 21% of the overrun and 2.3% of brake machine time. The overrun is spread across 1,980 part-operations, so no short list captures most of it. See Appendix Tables 25 and 26 for additional detail.

Handed-over setups carry 16.2 hours a week above the ratio of setups finished by the operator who started them, but most of that is length, not handover: a long setup is the one that reaches the end of the shift. Setups started in the last 30 minutes of a shift are handed over 79% of the time and run at a median 1.25 against 1.14 to 1.15 for setups started with more than an hour left. On that comparison the handover itself costs 1.2 brake hours a week. See Appendix Table 27 for additional detail.

Three levers release 12.9 brake hours a week, 4.1% of brake machine time: the top 12 part-operations run at standard (7.3 hours a week), repeat setups assigned to an operator who has set the part up before (4.4), and a shift-handover standard at the brakes, the operator who starts a setup finishing it or leaving a written handover at the machine (1.2). Options Tested carries these hours as the setup program.

4. Run Standards

L2 and L3 run at 0.81 of the run standard and L1 at 1.00; every other work center runs at 1.01 to 1.06. 553 of 1,931 active parts (29%) carry a stale standard (one dated at the part's first quote) and run 7% to 16% further above standard than refreshed parts at the same work center. See Appendix Tables 28 and 29 for additional detail.

Run Actual Over Standard by Work Center

With the stale standards refreshed, planned hours rise 1.7% at the brakes and 0.9% to 3.0% elsewhere; standard hours per job rise 8% on jobs for those parts (24% of jobs) and 1.9% overall. With the L2 and L3 run standards set to measured, planned laser hours fall 13.6% and standard hours per job fall 2.6%. Both together move standard hours per job by -0.7%. The 553 stale standards should be refreshed and the L2 and L3 run standards set to measured, with the effect on standard hours per job going to estimating (Quoting and Early Warning from Load). See Appendix Tables 30 and 31 for additional detail.
