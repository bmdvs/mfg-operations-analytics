Project 5 A3

Release control and the shop model

Custom sheet-metal fabrication job shop, about 120 employees, one plant. Shop model of the jobs released from January 2024 to December 2025; results for jobs shipped in 2025, whole year and Q2 to Q4.
Sources: ERP, shop-floor data collection, maintenance exports (batch 20261003T152921Z-20250101); 30 replications per scenario, 95% intervals. Lead time in working days, scheduled Saturdays counted.

Background and problem

In Q2 to Q4 of 2025 the 90th-percentile lead time was 18.2 working days against an average quote of 12.4, with 90.1% on time.
The first quarter, carrying the 2024 year-end build, shipped 40.8% on time and took the year to 76.6%.

Target

95% of jobs shipped by the promised date in every quarter.

Analysis

Change in on-time delivery and 90th-percentile lead time against current practice, by scenario, with 95% intervals.
  • A WIP cap at 240 to 180 jobs lowers on-time delivery by 3.7 to 19.6 points in Q2 to Q4; the floor already carries less WIP than the quotes allow.
  • Earliest due date makes no difference and critical ratio is 3.9 to 7.5 points worse.
  • The P4 setup reduction raises on-time delivery 4.3 points for the year and shortens the 90th percentile by 2.1 days.
  • A second shift on the robotic weld cell adds 0.8 points in Q2 to Q4.
  • Planned Saturdays from November through February add 1.8 points for the year; a lower bound, the model's peak being shallower than the shop's.

Expected results

Expected from the model, with 95% intervals: on-time delivery +6.6 points (+5.7 to +7.4) for the year and +1.2 points (+1.0 to +1.5) in Q2 to Q4; 90th-percentile lead time -3.3 days (-3.7 to -2.9) and -1.7 days (-1.9 to -1.5). The package does not reach the target on the promises as made; quoted lead times are P7.

Follow-up

Quarterly re-validation of the model against the actual lead time, WIP, utilization and on-time delivery. Saturday shifts and extended hours at the brakes reported monthly against the model's expected values.
