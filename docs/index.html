Flow and On-time DeliveryWhere does the lead time go, and does the floor's WIP match the quoted lead times? What makes jobs late, and do the shop's late-reason codes say so?Queue at the brakes is 28% of lead time and the stage that separates late jobs from on-time jobs (6.98 days against 2.71); the median job ships in 11.2 working days against an average quoted lead time of 12.4, 9.2 in Q2-Q4. In 2025, jobs waiting at work centers (i.e., queue constraints) accounted for 44% of the 4,647 lost days, while jobs that were released late accounted for 17%, and jobs that waited on material and outside processing accounted for 7% and 3%, respectively.Report
Capacity, Constraints and SetupsWhich work centers run hot, and how do their queues respond to load? What do setups cost at the brakes, and where is the overrun?In 2025, the brakes ran at 91% utilization against 97% uptime, this 6-point gap represents the smallest production capacity buffer of all work centers. The robotic weld cell ran at 83% utilization, the laser cutting stations at 74%, the assembly through hardware work centers ran at 66% to 69%, and the powder line ran at just 56% utilization. Brake setups ran 6,077 hours against 4,235 standard in 2025: 1,842 hours over, 35 a week, 11% of brake machine time.Report
Options TestedDo release control and dispatch rules help, and what does? Do the capital options pay back?A WIP cap and dispatch rules do not help; the setup program, a second shift on the robotic weld cell and planned Saturdays from November through February take the year to 88.0% on time, up 6.0 points (5.0 to 6.9), and Q2-Q4 to 91.8%. On overtime and labor alone no option pays back within the 7-year horizon, and the no-capital package is net negative ($70,000 a year, the weld cell's second shift). With half the released brake hours sold, the robotic cell pays back in 1.6 years, the tool changer on B3 in 3.9, the laser tower not at all.Report
Quoting and Early Warning from LoadWhat lead time should be quoted, and what wins quotes? Which weekly measures move before on-time delivery does?The fixed quote is met on 53.0% of non-rush jobs for the year and 70.5% in Q2-Q4; a quote by routing class and brake backlog at release, never below the fixed quote, is met on 67.0% and 82.1% out of sample and lengthens 43.6% and 33.9% of promised lead times. The on-time start rate correlates +0.62 with on-time delivery 1 week later, against a 95th percentile of +0.24 on shuffled series and +0.21 on shifted series. It moved before all four declines in on-time delivery; no other weekly measure leads in ordinary weeks.Report
